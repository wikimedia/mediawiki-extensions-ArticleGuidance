<instructions>
Articles about social issues — poverty, discrimination, homelessness, inequality, and similar problems affecting communities or societies — are appropriate when the issue is documented in reliable, independent sources such as academic research, government reports, or established journalism. Wikipedia articles on social issues must present multiple perspectives neutrally and be grounded in evidence. They should not advocate for a particular political position or serve as a platform for activism.

Quick considerations:
* Write from a neutral point of view. Social issues are often contested — present the range of documented perspectives without taking sides.
* Base the article on peer-reviewed research, government data, and established journalism. Avoid relying solely on advocacy organisations as sources.
* Distinguish between documented facts (statistics, research findings) and contested claims (causes, solutions, policy positions).
* Do not write the article as a call to action or use language that advocates for a particular political response.
* If the issue is primarily local or affects a specific country, make that scope clear in the introduction rather than presenting it as a universal phenomenon.

</instructions>
